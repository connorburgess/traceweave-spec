## Decision

**Recommendation (unexecuted, needs the owner):** if the owner opens IMG (IMG.0, or after M3.4), the first bounded experiment should be **extracted-fingerprint only: PDQ, with ISCC Image-Code as a second extractor**, on synthetic images, CPU-only, network-denied, with no model weights. It is the cheapest test that can fail meaningfully. It exercises the shared evidence record, the extraction contract and catalogue calibration for a second medium. It needs no weight licence and no generator. It also gives M2E.7 its fingerprint-only arm for images. The TrustMark mark study stays second, deferred, with the preconditions in the checkpoint note and below. A DWT-DCT mark is a negative control only, never a candidate.

**Strongest alternative:** run nothing on images until M3.4. The roadmap names this the default, and nothing here makes images urgent. **Confidence:** high for the measurement boundaries below; low for any prediction of PDQ or ISCC false-match rates on Traceweave catalogues. Neither has a published false-match measurement at a Traceweave-like threshold, catalogue size and query unit.

## Findings that change the earlier picture

1. **Fingerprints already have a documented false-match tail, but not at the thresholds we would use.** F2 measures FPR as P(∃ database image within T | query not in the database). That is the per-query, whole-catalogue unit M3.0 needs. Its PDQ thresholds (70/85/90) are far looser than Meta's starting recommendation (≤31, quality ≤49 discarded, F1 README). F1 itself measures precision only through 157 distinct originals (12,246 pairs) and visually inspected LabelMe clusters. **I:** no source supplies a PDQ rate at ≤31 for a large catalogue. It is our measurement to make, with the checkpoint's bound: 0 events in n independent negatives bounds the rate only near 3/n.
2. **Crop is the dividing line, and screenshot is unmeasured everywhere.** F1 places deep crops out of scope (random-pair distances). F3 says partial copies and collages are hard for global descriptors. F5's frequency method fails a 7×5 crop. TrustMark crop behaviour is in lane 08's sources, not here. No source in this review measures a screenshot transform. F1 mentions screenshots only as design motivation; F5's "screenshots" note concerns flat page content, not a capture pipeline. **I:** any image profile must say "whole-image, light-edit" scope, unless crop or screenshot is measured.
3. **Evasion is cheap for every non-learned hash.** F2's black-box attack used small L2 perturbations and needed only hash outputs. The cheapest removal for (a) is therefore either a deep crop (no tooling) or that black-box perturbation. For marks, F6 shows that off-the-shelf VAE regeneration removes three of four tested marks. ReMark already removes TrustMark (lane 08). **I:** absence of a match or mark never means "not derived".
4. **Presence, payload and identity separate differently for images.** F5 always returns bits, so "decoded" carries no presence evidence until a statistic and threshold are added; F6 uses ≥23/32 matching bits for p < 0.01. TrustMark's BCH success is the first point where a payload exists. Neither authenticates anything; 61 data bits come before any binding or signature cost. A fingerprint match is similarity to a catalogue entry, not a payload. **I:** the 08 container's raw/decoded/statistic split carries over unchanged.
5. **Transplant is a measured failure for TrustMark specifically.** F7's TrustMark-P baseline accepted 80–91% of authors' forgery attempts. Relation gating lowers this but does not reach zero, and it needs per-instance enrollment, which is reference-assisted verification. **I:** an image mark profile must record "decoded mark on unrelated host" as an expected outcome, as code already does.
6. **C2PA already names both our candidates.** `io.iscc.v0` and three TrustMark variants are registered soft-binding algorithms. Validation fails `c2pa.watermarked.bound` without a soft-binding assertion (§15.10.3.2.3). The 2.4 change history relaxes the algorithm-list requirement from shall to should. **I:** a Traceweave image record could later be exported as a soft-binding assertion, with no new algorithm id needed for ISCC or TrustMark. Registration grants no robustness or false-match standing.

## What a Traceweave image profile would have to specify

Proposal only; no `profiles/` or schema change.

- **Extraction contract (fingerprints):** decoder and version; EXIF orientation; ICC/colour-space conversion; alpha flattening colour; animated/multi-frame policy (first frame or reject); the luminance formula; the resize filter and target size; border-crop rule (ISCC requires one); hash algorithm and bit length; quality score and abstention threshold; dihedral variants (PDQ computes 8; F1 README warns that choosing a "minimal" one is inconsistent); distance metric; and vendor test vectors as conformance inputs (ThreatExchange `pdq/data`).
- **Carrier (marks):** algorithm and code commit; weight SHA-256; encode mode and ECC; strength and PSNR floor; channel and resolution handling; minimum size; the payload length check before the encoder (lane 08: `process_encode` truncates).
- **Detector:** threshold and its calibration identifier; search scope (catalogue size, dihedral variants, keys); presence statistic separate from decode; abstention reasons (low quality, too small, undecodable, unsupported mode).
- **Shared record fields, unchanged across media (from lane 08):** artifact digest and role; profile, configuration and model digests; region with coordinate system (pixels at a stated width × height after a stated orientation); raw value, decoded payload and statistic, separately; record resolution, signature validity and trust, separately. Confidence stays empty unless calibrated. Mark, fingerprint and signature evidence are never multiplied.
- **C2PA mapping (C2PA.1 input):** Traceweave artifact digest ↔ C2PA hard binding (mandatory, exact bytes). Fingerprint or decoded mark ↔ `c2pa.soft-binding` with `alg` from the list, `blocks[].value` and `scope.region`. Signature validity and trust ↔ claim-signature validation and trust lists, kept separate. **Unmapped in C2PA:** statistic, threshold, calibration id, abstention reason and search scope. These are verifier results, not manifest assertions. **Conflicting:** C2PA's region-map coordinates versus our pixel coordinates after orientation need a stated conversion. Pin version 2.4 and c2pa-rs (MIT/Apache-2.0) for C2PA.2 fixtures.

## Proposed first bounded experiment (unexecuted; needs the owner)

**E-IMG-F: PDQ and ISCC Image-Code on synthetic images.** It satisfies the checkpoint's deferred-study list item by item.

- **Image count.** 2,000 procedurally generated RGB images from a seeded generator committed with the protocol: random shapes, gradients and texture noise, at 512×512, 1024×768 and 1920×1080 (one third each). Split by generator seed: 500 threshold-selection, 500 evaluation catalogue, 1,000 evaluation negatives never enrolled. Each catalogue image gets 12 transforms: JPEG quality 90/75/50/30; resize 0.5 and 0.25 and back; centre crop to 90%, 75% and 50% area; horizontal flip; PNG round trip; a defined "screenshot" (pad with a 40 px UI-grey border, rescale ×0.8, PNG). That gives 6,000 positive queries and 1,000 negative queries against a 500-entry catalogue. Limitation: procedural images are not natural photos and are clustered by generator, so they **cannot** support rare-event rates. 0 false matches in 1,000 negatives bounds the per-query rate only below about 3×10⁻³.
- **Frozen before running (M3.3 style):** PDQ threshold 31 and quality floor 49 (vendor values); the ISCC threshold, chosen on the selection split only; the transform parameters; the decoder version.
- **Metrics with denominators:** per transform, positives matched to the correct entry / 500; wrong-entry matches / 500; abstentions; negatives matching any entry / 1,000; distance histograms; runtime and peak memory.
- **Failure criterion.** The study fails, and the profile is not proposed, if either holds: (i) any negative matches at the frozen threshold, or more than 5 wrong-entry matches across all 6,000 positives; (ii) recall below 95% for JPEG 75 or the PNG round trip, both claimed as in scope. Crop, screenshot and quality 30 results are recorded as profile limits, not failures.
- **Compute and disk (estimates, unmeasured; the owner bounds them):** CPU only, one laptop. Images regenerate from seeds and only hashes are stored, so under 1 GB of transient disk and under 50 MB committed. Wall clock is targeted under 2 h; the protocol records actual time and aborts at 4 h.
- **Weight licence:** none needed (no models). Dependency licences are pinned at install: ThreatExchange BSD at the ledger commit, iscc-core Apache-2.0, plus Pillow and NumPy terms to be read.
- **Download and install (owner):** a one-time install of Pillow, NumPy and iscc-core into a project-local virtual environment. ThreatExchange's pure-Python PDQ is vendored by commit. Nothing else.
- **Offline and CPU evidence:** run under `scripts/offline` (planned in the implementation repository; the probe must fail first). Hash the ThreatExchange `pdq/data` vectors and compare with that README's published outputs, as an implementation-conformance check.
- **Follow-on, separate approval:** E-IMG-M (TrustMark, per IMG.1–IMG.5 and the checkpoint note), with F5 `dwtDct` as a no-key negative control, F6-style VAE regeneration only if a VAE weight licence is accepted, and a residual-transplant arm.

## After the report audit

Added 2026-11-22 after the [report audit](10-report-audit.md), which checked three more primary sources (X1–X3). The audit's decisive source, X1, is an author-run preprint by a provenance vendor. Its results are author measurements, not independent replication. The rows above are left as first written; this section supersedes them where it disagrees.

**What changes.**
- **Fingerprint gaps.** Three "N" cells for PDQ and ISCC now have author evidence. PDQ at a held-out pair-level 10⁻⁷ threshold gave 267 false bindings in 26,820 never-registered queries (1.00% per query) against a 98,985-item registry dominated by product near-copies. Both hashes fail 70%-side crops and screenshot templates. PDQ and ISCC were bit-identical across x86 and arm64 on 200 files.
- **E-IMG-F's purpose.** It would now mostly reproduce known direction. Its failure criterion (i), "any negative match", is miscalibrated for natural images. Revised proposal: a smaller **contract-and-record conformance run**. It would use 200 seeded synthetic images, the ThreatExchange vendor vectors and the same transforms. False-match counts would be reported without a pass line. It **fails** if vendor vectors mismatch, if outputs differ between two runs or between the owner's x86 and arm64 hosts, or if a result does not fit the shared record fields. Budget, installs and offline evidence are unchanged.
- **Mark preconditions.** A BCH-valid TrustMark decode is not presence evidence: 5.9–15.3% of unmarked images yield one. Any mark arm uses an expected-payload k-of-n test on transmitted bits, and the k is recorded with its analytic per-key bound and its observed per-source bound. With N keys, the bound is N×10⁻⁶.
- **PixelSeal joins TrustMark.** It becomes a co-candidate for the deferred mark study: post-hoc, 256 bits, MIT code and README-stated MIT weights. Its owner items are the weight download, SA-1B training terms, and a removal/transplant arm that no checked source supplies.

**What does not change.** Order and gating stay as they were: no image work before M3.4 unless the owner approves IMG.0; the fingerprint run first, because it needs no weights; the mark study second and owner-gated. Cross-host bit flips were measured in video, not still images, so no colour-pipeline mandate follows. The report's mandates, gates and "peer-reviewed" framing are rejected (audit Flags).
