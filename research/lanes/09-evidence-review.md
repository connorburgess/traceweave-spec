## Decision support

**Recommendation (unexecuted, for coordinator reconciliation):** the only candidate that merits a bounded experiment is an **extracted-fingerprint text profile hosted by the existing winnowing contract**: word tokens after a declared Unicode fold, winnowed k-grams, original UTF-8 byte spans, and boilerplate suppression by catalogue. It needs no model or weight download, runs on CPU by construction (SHA-256 and string processing), reuses the contract's hashing, selection and Unicode 17.0.0 tables, and its failure boundary (paraphrase, translation, short fragments) is already documented in primary sources. **No embedded mark in existing text merits an experiment now.** Unicode carriers are erased by one published deterministic fold (N, §§6–7; U, UTS #39 §4), which is the same "cheap normalization" failure that ended the Go/Rust carriers. The reviewed post-hoc lexical rewriter (AWT) carries about 4 bits per 80 words, changes the author's wording, has no licence file and needs several third-party model checkpoints.

Strongest alternative: defer all text work until M3.4, as the roadmap orders for media lanes; a text profile is not on the roadmap and starting it needs a recorded owner override. Confidence: high on the bounded source observations below; low on any transfer to real prose corpora, which nothing here measured.

## Fingerprint comparison for prose

**Tokenization and normalization.** W and B both assume a format-specific front end that reduces a document to a canonical token sequence (B §2; W §5.2: MOSS keeps per-format front ends separate from the fingerprinting engine). M additionally uses case folding, stop-word removal, stemming and phrase detection (§2), which are language-dependent. **Inference:** a Traceweave text profile should keep the language-independent part (Unicode fold, word segmentation) in the profile and exclude stemming and stop-word lists from the first version, because they need per-language data whose version must also be pinned. UAX #29 word boundaries ignore zero-width joiners and format characters (WB4), so a zero-width insertion does not split a word token; the fold must still delete Default_Ignorable code points or the token's exact text changes.

**Paraphrase and translation limits.** No reviewed source measures winnowing or shingling recall under paraphrase or translation. K shows BM25 lexical retrieval with ≥ 50-token queries still finds model-paraphrased passages in a catalogue of the originals, and fails below ~20 words; that is retrieval scored by unigram overlap, a weaker requirement than a shared winnowed k-gram. P's corpus validation shows longer word n-gram similarity falling for obfuscated cases (Fig. 1). **Inference:** a k-gram profile should be expected to miss heavy paraphrase and all translation; a bag-of-words or retrieval channel would be a separate, later profile. Cross-language matching needs translation or multilingual models and is out of scope for a CPU-only, no-download profile.

**Common-phrase and boilerplate suppression.** W §5.1 finds the most frequent k-grams are menus, legal boilerplate and repetitive strings; W §5.2 suppresses boilerplate by fingerprinting it under a special document ID. This is the explicit-catalogue suppression of the [draft catalogue contract](../drafts/catalogue-report-contract.md) (OD-C5 option b, roadmap M2.10). For prose, licence texts, front matter, Markdown tables of contents and quoted standards text are the obvious boilerplate classes.

**Calibration evidence.** None of W, B or M gives a false-alert rate with a negative-set denominator. M's 0.75 precision/recall is per sampled pair on web pages. K's 1% FPR is chosen on the evaluation scores. P supplies negative documents and graded obfuscation, but the 2010 paper reports corpus construction, not detector error. Per the [lane 07 review](07-evidence-review.md), any rate must be computed over the full query (all windows and catalogue entries), with thresholds frozen before held-out data.

## Embedded marks in existing text

**Unicode carriers (N, U).** Two tracks: substitution (homoglyphs and compatibility variants) and insertion (ZERO WIDTH SPACE, ZWNJ, ZWJ, WORD JOINER in gaps). N measures raw capacity, then effective capacity after each transformation (§3 Table 1, §6). Survival depends on the track: modelled copy-paste removes the whole insertion track in all 40 varieties; NFKC removes 96% of compatibility-variant pairs but keeps 93% of cross-script pairs; skeleton mapping folds listed confusables; the "combined" canonical fold (compatibility decomposition, skeleton, White_Space → U+0020, delete default-ignorables) erases the mark in every language (§§6–7). App. E reports that keying the detector on raw received characters is self-defeating (13.9% of labels survive attack), so the authors key on the canonical fold. **Inference:** the detector's own keying already applies the removal function, so any profile that folds before extraction cannot see the mark. Visibility depends on fonts and shaping engines (§6, App. D). **Inference, not measured here:** substituted or inserted characters can also disturb search, spell checking and screen readers. Reflow, editors, Markdown rendering and LLM paraphrase were not measured; paraphrase or retyping replaces characters and should be assumed to erase both tracks.

**Synonym and syntactic carriers (A).** AWT rewrites existing text with a learned model. Aggregating segments with a binomial test turns bit accuracy into a presence decision (§V-B4). It is closer to identity than a presence-only mark, because it decodes a message, but 4 raw bits per 80 words before ECC, versioning and authentication leaves little for an identity payload. Robustness numbers are against random edits, a denoising autoencoder and re-marking, not against LLM paraphrase or translation.

**Formatting carriers in Markdown/HTML.** No primary source in this review measures Markdown or HTML formatting carriers (emphasis-marker choice, list markers, reference vs inline links, line-break placement). **Inference, untested:** Markdown has many byte-different spellings that render identically, but any CommonMark-to-HTML render, formatter or "copy as plain text" collapses them, so they would fail the cheapest-normalizer test in the same way the Go/Rust syntax carriers did. Treat this as an unchecked hypothesis, not a finding.

**Post-hoc vs generation-time.** Lane 08's text representative (Kirchenbauer) is a generation-time presence test. N and A are post-hoc and apply to text a creator already wrote; neither is a signature, and neither has been tested for transplantation of the marked characters or words into another document.

## How the extraction contract could host a text profile

The [draft contract](../drafts/extraction-contract.md) already separates lexing (§§4–5) from serialization, hashing and winnowing (§§6–8) and pins Unicode 17.0.0 tables (§3a). A text profile would add one lexer section and reuse the rest. It would have to specify, with no TBDs:

- **Input and format front end.** UTF-8 rules of §3; whether a BOM is rejected (as now) or skipped, since text editors write BOMs routinely; a `format` value (`plain` or `markdown`) with a pinned CommonMark version if Markdown syntax is dropped, as MOSS keeps front ends separate (W §5.2).
- **Fold.** An exact pipeline over the pinned 17.0.0 files: NFKC, full case folding, delete Default_Ignorable_Code_Point, map White_Space to a separator, and optionally a confusable channel with a pinned `confusables.txt` digest (UTS #39 §4 warns the data changes). Name the Unicode version in the profile, not the runtime's: N reports Python's library used Unicode 14.0 data.
- **Tokens.** UAX #29 word boundaries or a simpler declared rule (maximal letter/number runs); a declared rule for scripts written without spaces, which this review did not check.
- **Channels.** `exact` (token as written) and `folded` (as above), matching the contract's two-channel shape; no stemming or stop words in the first version.
- **Span mapping.** Folding changes lengths (NFKC maps the one-character ligature U+FB01 to two letters; case folding can expand). Every folded token must carry the original half-open byte span; this is the main new requirement and needs vectors.
- **Parameters.** Word k and window frozen in the configuration bytes; W used k = 50 characters, w = 100 on web data, P's rationale cites 8-word shingles. These are reference points, not choices.
- **Errors.** A closed list like §4.8 (`invalid-utf8`, `bom` or not, `nul`, `input-too-large`, `unsupported-config`, plus any Markdown front-end failure).
- **Hash framing.** The §7 length-prefixed bytes with a new contract string such as `traceweave-text-winnow-draft-1`; the hash stays unkeyed and gives no privacy.

**Shared evidence fields across media** (from the lane 08 container, unchanged): artifact digest and role, profile/version/config digest, coordinate system (here UTF-8 byte spans of the original), raw evidence and decode result as separate fields, statistic with calibration identifier or none, abstention/insufficient-evidence status, and record, signature and trust results kept separate. A text profile adds no new shared field; the fold and span mapping stay profile-specific. Fragment containment and whole-document similarity stay separate fields (M2.5).

## Proposed first bounded experiment (unexecuted; needs the owner)

**Question.** Can the existing contract host a `text` profile whose fold and span mapping two independent implementations reproduce byte for byte, and what does it match on human-obfuscated prose?

**Stage 1, no download.** Inputs: the repository's own Markdown prose (11 files under `docs/`, `spec/` and `profiles/`, about 10,000 words, CC BY 4.0 per [LICENSE.md](../../LICENSE.md)) plus hand-written synthetic vectors. Deterministic transformations, each applied to every file: reflow, CRLF, NFC, NFKC, case change, zero-width insertion every 10th gap, a listed-confusable substitution, Markdown-to-plain stripping, smart quotes, insertion into an unrelated 20× larger host, an excerpt of exactly k + w − 1 tokens, and one distractor word inserted after every k + w − 2 tokens and, separately, after every k − 1 tokens. Both are recorded limits, not failures: spacing k + w − 2 removes only the locality guarantee (the [text extraction review](../reviews/extraction-profile-text-review.md) (§5, commit `c6e8f2a`) measured 326 of 807 distinct hashes still shared at k = 8, w = 8), and only spacing ≤ k − 1, which leaves no unchanged k-gram, is expected to share 0 fingerprints. Negatives: each file against every other file (11 × 10 ordered pairs) and the boilerplate set. Budget: CPU only, under 10 minutes, under 50 MB disk (estimate, not measured). **Failure criterion:** any vector where two implementations differ; any reported span that does not equal the original bytes of the matched tokens; any transformed positive with an unchanged folded run ≥ k + w − 1 tokens that shares 0 fingerprints. Any of these stops Stage 2.

**Stage 2, one download.** PAN-PC-10 ([Zenodo record 3250123](https://zenodo.org/records/3250123), CC BY 4.0 on the record, 1,773,941,212 bytes across its files): a fixed, seeded sample of 200 cases per obfuscation class (none, low, high, simulated, translated: 1,000 positives) and 200 suspicious documents without plagiarism as negatives. Report per class: case detection with the PAN measures (P §2), abstentions, and negative documents with any match, each with its denominator. Freeze k, w and boilerplate catalogue before running; no threshold is chosen and no rate is claimed beyond what n supports (0 events in 200 negatives bounds the rate only below about 1.5%). Budget: CPU only, estimated under 1 hour and under 5 GB disk including the archive (unmeasured). **Recorded as profile limits, not failures:** translated and simulated cases are expected to be missed.

**Everything that needs the owner:** a use case for text, written like UC-6 (as IMG.0 does for images); a recorded override of the roadmap's "media lanes wait for M3.4"; whether the repository's own prose may be used as fixtures; the PAN-PC-10 download, its licence acceptance and where it is stored (Gutenberg-derived texts; the record's licence was read from the Zenodo API, the archive's own notices were not checked); a CommonMark version pin; any new roadmap gate rows (this lane does not edit ROADMAP.md); and the draft contract's pending OD-C5 option. No model, weight or paid service is required by either stage.

## Claim-audit checklist for the incoming NotebookLM report

Unchecked items are future checks, not findings about a report that has not arrived.

- [ ] Separate extracted fingerprints, post-hoc marks, generation-time marks and signatures; do not present Kirchenbauer-style results as marking existing text.
- [ ] For each fingerprint method, state unit (characters, words, tokens), k, window or sketch size, hash width, fold, and catalogue size; reject headline accuracy without a negative-set denominator.
- [ ] Keep M's 0.75 precision/recall per sampled web-page pair; do not restate it as a false-alert rate for fragments.
- [ ] Keep K's retrieval results at ≥ 50-token queries and test-set operating points; do not extend them to short excerpts, human prose or k-gram winnowing.
- [ ] Demand a measurement, with settings, for any claim that winnowing or shingling survives paraphrase or translation.
- [ ] For Unicode marks, separate raw bits per character from authenticated payload; state key, ECC and framing overhead; say whether the detector needs the original cover.
- [ ] Separate modelled copy-paste from measured editor, browser or clipboard round trips; name the application and version for any measured one.
- [ ] Require the canonical-fold (NFKC + skeleton + delete default-ignorables) result for any Unicode-carrier survival claim.
- [ ] Pin the Unicode version and confusables data; reject claims based on a runtime's unstated Unicode data.
- [ ] For lexical rewriting marks, state bits per word count, meaning-preservation metric and whether humans judged it; keep re-marking and paraphrase attacks separate.
- [ ] Treat any Markdown/HTML formatting-carrier survival claim as unsupported unless it cites a measured render or formatter round trip.
- [ ] Check licences of code, weights and corpora separately; require evidence before "runs offline on CPU".
- [ ] Do not infer authorship, ancestry, ownership or training membership from a mark, a fingerprint match or a retrieval hit.
- [ ] Label every proposed profile, gate, threshold or schema field as unadopted; report-proposed names stay quarantined.

## After the report audit

The [report audit](09-report-audit.md) (2026-11-22) leaves the recommendation unchanged: one bounded fingerprint experiment on the winnowing contract, no embedded-mark experiment. The report reached the same decision, but its main reason for rejecting Unicode marks, that NFKC strips zero-width characters and homoglyphs, is contradicted; the canonical fold in N and UTS #39 is the removal that works, as this review already states. Its experiment is not adopted: it needs a corpus download, sets invented thresholds, expects containment ≥ 0.98 under 1% word substitution where k = 8 implies roughly 0.92, and tests a dilution identity that holds by definition. Two changes follow. Stage 1 now includes periodic word insertion as a recorded limit. Correction (2026-11-22): spacing w + k − 2, the report's "cheapest removal", defeats only the guarantee; the [text extraction review](../reviews/extraction-profile-text-review.md) (§5, commit `c6e8f2a`) measured 326 of 807 distinct hashes still shared at k = 8, w = 8, and sharing reached zero only at one insertion every k − 1 = 7 tokens. Full removal needs spacing ≤ k − 1. fpcc is not a usable prose reference implementation: it lexes C source (audit C3). The background-frame manuscript (audit C2) supports the existing explicit boilerplate catalogue (OD-C5 option b, M2.10) and adds no field. Owner items are unchanged.
