## Findings requiring author disposition

### R1 — define registration cancellation at signing and storage boundaries

Proposal §2 includes R creation/signing and supplied-input verification; §5 asks for distinct same-matrix results if shipped, with existing key/consent/storage boundaries unchanged. P7 describes terminating disposable computation, invalidating job IDs and discarding late results. It does not specify whether those deadlines apply to R, or how cancellation is reported after key persistence, signing, or card persistence. “Boundaries unchanged” does not define a cancellation outcome at those boundaries. This is conditional on shipping R, but the scope ambiguity exists in the proposed design now.

Concrete static evidence at Shelf `86036c43394e94c1431ca335195a86e7754da6df`: `src/components/RegisterTab.tsx:201–245` awaits `onEnsureKey`, then `createSignedRegistrationBundle`, then `runOfflineVerify`, then calls `onCardCreated`. `src/components/ShelfApp.tsx:84–87,122–125` delegates key creation and saves the resulting card. `src/lib/storage.ts:116–126` generates and saves a missing key before registration signing; `:32–34,49–51` writes cards/keys to localStorage. `src/lib/crypto.ts:147` awaits `crypto.subtle.sign`. These are static source facts, not claims of observed races or source-to-deployment identity.

A cancel arriving during verification can therefore follow a persisted key and a completed signature, before card persistence; one arriving after the card callback cannot undo that write by discarding a worker response. This is a concrete reachable sequence in the source, not a numeric experiment. [Worker.terminate documentation](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/API/Worker/terminate) supports stopping a worker without cleanup; it supplies no transaction rollback for main-thread storage or an already completed signature.

Smallest correction: explicitly scope P7 to disposable F/C/V work and hold R cancellation for a separately reviewed protocol, **or** define R phase/commit outcomes now. If included, identify pre-key, pre-sign/in-flight sign, post-sign/pre-store and post-store states; distinguish suppressing future work from reversing an effect; retain truthful committed/completed/error outcomes and receipts. Do not claim “cancelled with no effects” after a commit, silently discard a saved result, automatically create another key, or retry signing as recovery. Any uncancellable commit boundary needs explicit UI and acceptance rules rather than inheriting an impossible rollback promise. This recommendation changes no consent, key, policy or storage default.

### R2 — make the cancellation denominator unique

P7 says “10/10 cancel probes at early/mid/late active phases per subpath/cell.” This admits ten probes distributed over three phases or ten probes in **each** phase. Across four core subpaths and two required devices those readings produce 80 versus 240 planned probes before any further profile/phase expansion. Neither a pooled 10/10 nor a pooled desktop/mobile maximum establishes per-device acceptance.

Smallest correction: select and spell out one allocation, with a planned count for every applicable `(subpath, device, phase)` and any exposed-profile expansion, plus the total. For example, ten per phase gives 30/30 per subpath/device and 240/240 across the minimal four-by-two matrix, assuming exactly three applicable phases. If fewer or additional real phases are intended, enumerate them before freeze. Report planned/measured/passed for each cell and preserve failures/unavailable cells. Do not count an already-finished task as an active cancellation success. The existing requirement to preselect cancellable workloads/triggers is appropriate; feasibility of doing so within the proposed small caps remains unmeasured and must be resolved before a conformance run, not by reducing the denominator after collection.

## Accepted bounds and source dispositions

- **Temporal amendment and unchanged clauses:** ROADMAP WEB.6 still has the original pre-WEB.1 sentence and planned status. The other three criterion sentences, including keyboard k/k and the exact limit/one-byte-over condition, occur verbatim in the proposal. The missed-prerequisite statement is explicitly bounded to available recorded evidence, neither exhaustive absence nor retrospective compliance. Replacing that timing requirement is an owner decision, not a finding that the original requirement was met.
- **Counts and targets:** F/C/V retain 3/3 flows and four mandatory subpaths; both C paths remain mandatory, missing V stays in the denominator, and registration verification cannot replace replay. D and R have separate counts. P4 explicitly requires ten interactions per control/subpath/device; P8 requires five per workload/profile/subpath/device. P2's five cold/five warm runs and P3's five first-use runs need to be expanded explicitly for each required cell in the frozen manifest. Small all-run lab samples and maxima are useful acceptance observations, not field percentile or population reliability estimates. D3 is diagnostic, never a replacement physical phone. No feasibility or device availability is certified here.
- **Source fidelity:** [INP](https://web.dev/articles/inp) and [LCP](https://web.dev/articles/lcp) support their respective good field thresholds and p75 interpretation, not whole-job timing or the proposed all-run thresholds. [Long-task guidance](https://web.dev/articles/optimize-long-tasks) identifies tasks over 50 ms; zero attributable tasks is a project target. The ledger correctly distinguishes the dated [WCAG 2.2 Recommendation](https://www.w3.org/TR/2024/REC-WCAG22-20241212/) from informative techniques and numerical engineering choices. The three reported web.dev update dates match the consulted pages. No copied research table is treated as authority.
- **Progress:** P6 separates at-most-4/s visual updates and an honest heartbeat from actual engine progress; P13 limits coarse real-progress announcements to once per ten seconds and suppresses unchanged duplicates. [ARIA25](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Techniques/aria/ARIA25) supports separate live status because progressbar is not itself a live region; it mandates neither this cadence nor one markup implementation. Phase/start/terminal speech and actual DOM/AT observations must be enumerated in the later script; those selected tests are not whole-WCAG certification.
- **Bytes and memory:** exact UTF-8 bytes, aggregate limits, entry counts, L/L+1 and N/N+1 are explicit; proposed chunks do not imply streaming tokenization. Static implementation `dcaa0d42e9de63d23aa113d9306134de9b28942c` confirms the 8,388,608-byte hard extraction cap and complete-buffer synchronous wrapper, not a browser support envelope. Shelf's selected 1,048,576-byte config is distinct. Historical 1,128,365-byte WASM evidence supplies no new transfer measurement. [Transferable buffers](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/API/Web_Workers_API/Transferable_objects) do not remove parser or WASM-copy accounting. [Memory API documentation](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/API/Performance/measureUserAgentSpecificMemory) supports the secure/isolation and implementation-dependent limitations. A ledger or sampled process value cannot certify whole-context peak memory; the proposed ceiling remains unresolved before freeze.
- **Final delta accepted:** a2f867d adds only per-context observer detection, equivalent-trace requirements and explicit unmeasured-cell handling plus L18. [supportedEntryTypes](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/API/PerformanceObserver/supportedEntryTypes_static) distinguishes available entry types across contexts. The draft correctly requires collection completeness/precision review beyond availability and retains planned/measured/passed. Heartbeat, rAF, shell-ready and buffer-ledger evidence cannot replace paint, LCP, interaction or total-memory quantities. Empty buffers cannot establish zero tasks. Reviewed equivalent traces must prove the same quantity and phase coverage; their availability on D1/D2 is not established by this review.
- **Offline and permissions:** P14 begins only after explicit asset readiness and counts failed request attempts. Same-document repeats, HTTP freshness, offline reload and first-ever offline use remain separate. [HTTP caching](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/HTTP/Guides/Caching) and [service-worker lifecycle](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/API/Service_Worker_API/Using_Service_Workers) do not supply guaranteed cache retention. No telemetry, service worker, isolation header, storage permission, hosting change or runtime execution is authorized by the proposal. Broader browser support is not inferred from Chrome diagnostics.
