## 1. Question, and what the run does and does not show

**Question.** For two post-hoc image marks, TrustMark-Q and PixelSeal, does an **expected-payload test** behave as its analysis predicts on unmarked, wrong-key and wrong-entry inputs, and which edits, removal attempts and forgeries does it survive or accept, on one host, offline, on CPU?

This is the first study that tests the project's original goal directly: a recoverable mark embedded in content (UC-6, and UC-7 for images).

**What it shows if it passes:**
- The pinned code, weights and environment embed and recover a key-derived payload offline on CPU on this host, deterministically across two runs.
- On 100 synthetic images per scheme, clean marked images are accepted for their own entry at least 95 times in 100 (P1).
- No unmarked image, wrong key or wrong entry crosses the threshold in 39,900 comparisons per scheme (P2–P4). That is consistent with the analytic per-comparison bound of §5. It does not measure that bound.
- Counts, with denominators, for 12 edits, 5 removal attempts, residual transplant and overwrite.

**What it does not show:**
- **Anything about natural images.** Synthetic images are shapes, gradients, low-frequency texture and noise. They have no faces, text, fine natural texture or camera noise, and the learned marks were trained on natural photographs. A result here may be better or worse than on photographs, and no direction is predicted (§13, §16).
- **A false-accept rate.** 0 in 100 unmarked images bounds the per-source rate only below 2.95% (§13). The 10⁻⁶ figure is analytic and per comparison.
- **Authentication, ownership or ancestry.** A decoded payload is not signed. Anyone with the public decoder can read raw bits. A transplanted or forged mark can be accepted (§7, forgery arm), and that is a measured limit, not a pass or fail.
- **Robustness to adaptive or learned attacks** beyond the authors' own ReMark remover. No VAE or diffusion regeneration and no WmForger (lane 12).
- **Equality across hosts.** One macOS arm64 host; cross-host is not tested.

## 4. Schemes, payloads and keys

**Keys.** Two study keys: K_A = SHA-256("traceweave e-img-m-1 study key A (public test key)") and K_B likewise with "B" (`twmark/payload.py`). They are **public test values**. The study tests the statistic, not key secrecy. A deployment would draw a secret random key per creator (UC-7).

**Payload.** For key K, scheme s and entry id e, the bits are the first n bits, most significant first, of HMAC-SHA256(K, "e-img-m-1|s|e").

**Identity binding.** The payload is a keyed function of the catalogue entry id. A verifier holding the key recomputes the expected bits for each entry and tests agreement (§5). Nothing else binds identity: there is no signature over the payload, no binding to the image's content, and no version or framing field of ours.

| Scheme | What is embedded | Tested bits n | Settings |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `tm` TrustMark-Q | 61 data bits = the payload with n = 61, BCH_5-encoded by upstream `DataLayer` into 100 transmitted bits (61 data + 35 ECC + 4 version). The adapter checks the length before calling the encoder, because upstream `process_encode` silently truncates or pads (lane 08) | 100 transmitted bits | `use_ECC=True`, `encoding_type=BCH_5`, `WM_STRENGTH=1.0`, `WM_MERGE='bilinear'`, `concentrate_wm_region=1.0`, `device='cpu'`, upstream `encode(…, MODE='binary')` |
| `ps` PixelSeal | the 256-bit payload | 256 | model card as pinned (`scaling_w` 0.2, JND attenuation `jnd_1_1`, `img_size_proc` 256); `embed(…, is_video=False)` with upstream default interpolation; output `round(clamp(x, 0, 1) × 255)` half to even |
| `dw` `dwtDct` (control) | the 32-bit payload, via `set_watermark('bits', …)` | 32 | library defaults (`scales=[0,36,36]`, `block=4`); RGB↔BGR swapped at the boundary |

**Raw bits** (`raw_bits`), before any error correction:
- `tm`: upstream `decode()` up to the sign of the decoder output. That is `get_the_image_for_processing`, a BILINEAR resize to 245×245, the decoder network, then `> 0`. There is no detector, rotation search or BCH. A unit test checks that BCH-decoding these bits equals upstream `decode(MODE='binary')`.
- `ps`: upstream `detect(…, is_video=False)`, then `preds[0, 1:] > 0`, averaged over space first if the output is spatial.
- `dw`: upstream `WatermarkDecoder('bits', 32).decode(…, 'dwtDct')`.

**TrustMark's blind BCH result** (`bch.valid`, `bch.dataEqualsTrue`) is recorded for every TrustMark query. It **never** enters the decision (lane 10 "After the report audit": a BCH-valid decode is not presence evidence).

**Marked artifact.** The marked array is encoded as PNG (`compress_level` 6), then decoded again. That PNG is the published artifact; every condition starts from its decoded pixels.

**No download at run time.** Upstream TrustMark fetches missing model files; the adapter replaces that method with one that refuses. PixelSeal is built with upstream `setup_model` from the local checkpoint, never `videoseal.load`. Every model file's size and SHA-256 are checked before it is loaded.

**Import-only dependencies.** `av` and `pycocotools` are replaced by fail-on-use stub modules before videoseal is imported (lane 12). Any attribute use raises `StubUsed`, which would abort the run.

## 5. Decision rule and false-accept analysis

**Statistic.** For raw bits r and expected bits e of the same length n: the agreement m = #{i : rᵢ = eᵢ}.

**Threshold.** Accept iff m ≥ k(n), where k(n) is the smallest k with P(Bin(n, ½) ≥ k) ≤ 10⁻⁶, computed exactly with integers (`payload.k_for`). Frozen values:

| Scheme | n | k | P(Bin(n, ½) ≥ k) | at k − 1 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `tm` | 100 | 74 | 8.34 × 10⁻⁷ | 2.35 × 10⁻⁶ |
| `ps` | 256 | 167 | 6.22 × 10⁻⁷ | 1.18 × 10⁻⁶ |
| `dw` | 32 | 30 | 1.23 × 10⁻⁷ | 1.28 × 10⁻⁶ |

The rule reproduces the k values X1 used (74/100, 167/256; [audit](../lanes/10-report-audit.md)), but it was chosen as a rule, not copied: the 10⁻⁶ target is the audit's per-key figure.

**Search.** Each query is tested against all N = 100 entries of one key (key A, except where §7 says key B). The query outcome is:
- `match` if exactly one entry is accepted;
- `ambiguous` if two or more are;
- `no-match` if none is;
- `abstain-<code>` if the bytes cannot be decoded (`undecodable`), are not 8-bit RGB (`unsupported-mode`), or are below a scheme's minimum size (`too-small`; `dwtDct` refuses under 256×256 pixels).

A positive is **correct** when the outcome is `match` and the entry is its own.

**False-accept analysis.**
- **One key, one expected payload, any input.** The payload is an HMAC output that the input image and the decoder never see. So for any fixed raw string r, including the structured outputs X1 saw from unmarked images, the agreement with a payload under a uniformly random key is exactly Bin(n, ½). P(accept) = P(Bin(n, ½) ≥ k) ≤ 10⁻⁶ per comparison. The probability is over the key, not over images, and it assumes HMAC-SHA256 behaves as a random function.
- **N catalogue entries under one key.** Per query, P(any accept) ≤ N × 10⁻⁶ by the union bound: ≤ 10⁻⁴ for N = 100.
- **N_K keys** (several creators, or key rotation): ≤ N_K × N × 10⁻⁶ per query.
- **Many queries under one fixed key are not independent trials.** If the decoder returns nearly the same string for many unmarked images, and that string happens to agree with some entry's payload at ≥ k bits, all of those images are accepted together. The analytic bound still holds for each comparison and by the union bound over all of them: with C comparisons, P(any accept) ≤ C × 10⁻⁶. But the observed count is clustered. This is why the record keeps the number of distinct raw strings among unmarked images and the maximum agreement with any entry (`agreeMaxOtherHistogram`). The observed per-source bound is reported separately (§13), as the audit requires.
- **What the analysis does not cover.** A marked image's decoded bits are not independent of its own payload, so the bound says nothing about overwrite, transplant or forgery, where an attacker copies or re-embeds a real mark. Those are measured counts (§7) with no bound. Nor does it cover a decoder bias that coincides with how payloads are derived, which HMAC rules out unless HMAC is broken.

| ID | Pass line, per candidate scheme | Justification before data |
| --- | --- | --- |
| P1 | clean marked images correctly matched ≥ **95 of 100** | Both authors report near-perfect clean recovery on natural images (lane 08 I; X3). | P2 | unmarked inputs accepted for any entry: **0** in 20,000 comparisons (100 covers + 100 hosts, × 100 entries) | Union bound over 20,000 comparisons ≤ 0.02 (§5). One accept would contradict the per-comparison analysis at that level |

The verdict per candidate is **met** if W1–W6 hold and P1–P4 all pass. If W1–W6 hold and any P fails, it is **negative-result**, and the failing line is reported. For P1, a negative result means "fails on this synthetic set", nothing more. Neither verdict says anything about natural images.

**Report-only, no pass line.** All other counts: the 12 edits, the 5 removal attempts, transplant, overwrite, TrustMark blind-BCH validity, the quality figures and the negative control. There is no basis before data for a survival rate on synthetic images, and inventing one would repeat the error the lane 10 audit flagged (invented gates).

**Stated expectations, not pass lines** (written down so that the result can be compared with them):
- E1: transplant is accepted for most TrustMark and PixelSeal entries. The residual is added, not bound to content (lane 10 finding 5; F7 reports 80–91% for TrustMark-P against learned forgers).
- E2: ReMark removes most TrustMark marks (lane 08: 55% bit accuracy at 48.5 dB).
- E3: `dwtDct` fails the expected-payload test on many conditions where the candidates pass. That is its purpose as a control.
- E4: overwrite with key B makes the key-B payload accepted and the original key-A payload rejected for most TrustMark entries (TrustMark FAQ: TrustMark does not co-exist with itself).

**PSNR floor.** 38.0 dB between the cover and the marked image, over all RGB samples. The floor is 4 dB under the lowest author or X1 mean for the candidates (TrustMark-Q about 42–43 dB). It marks a marked image that UC-7 would refuse to publish. It is a policy line, not a calibrated perceptual threshold. Marked images below it are counted (`quality.belowFloor`) and **still tested**, so every denominator stays 100. The report lists how many successes come from images below the floor.

`overwrite` original and new share one set of bytes, and `wrongkey` reuses the `clean` decode. Each entry therefore has 24 query lines, counting `cover` and `host` once per entry. **Per scheme: 2,400 query lines** = 100 × 22 conditions (1–22) + 100 covers + 100 hosts. **Across the three schemes: 7,200.**

**Forgery and transplant, what counts.** For `transplant`, `acceptedAsTrue` counts forged images accepted as the donor's entry: the attacker's success. For `overwrite` it is reported both ways. The wrong-key condition reuses the clean decode, so it needs no extra decode.

**Abort rule.**
- A run whose elapsed time exceeds **4 h**, about 3 times the estimate, stops at the next query. It writes only `summary.json` with status `aborted-time-limit` and no counts, and exits with status 3.
- An aborted run is not a result. It may be repeated under the same version on a less loaded machine.
- If two consecutive runs abort, the owner decides what to do, and any change is a new version.
- A run that fails for memory is reported as failed.

**Mapping to the lane 08 shared record**, and what a mark record needs that the fingerprint record (profile §8.3) lacks:

| Lane 08 shared field | Fingerprint record (`e-img-f-2`) | Mark record (`e-img-m-1`) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| artifact digest and role | `sha256`; catalogue entry or query | `sha256` of the query; plus, per entry, the **cover** and **marked** digests. Marking creates a second artifact, so both roles exist |
| profile, configuration, model digests | configuration digests; no model | **model digests** (`weights.sha256`, 6 files) and the source manifest; scheme settings in §4. There is no configuration-bytes digest yet (open item O1) |
| region and coordinate system | whole image, pixels after orientation | whole image; no localisation (TrustMark's bounding-box detector is not used) |
| raw value | the hash | `rawBits`: the decoder's transmitted-bit estimate |
| decoded payload | none | TrustMark `bch` result, kept separate and never decisive; PixelSeal and `dwtDct` have none |
| statistic | Hamming distance | **agreement m of n with an expected payload**: a different statistic, which needs a name and an n |
| threshold and calibration basis | vendor value, uncalibrated | **k, with its basis**: "analytic, P(Bin(n, ½) ≥ k) ≤ 10⁻⁶ per comparison", identified by this protocol's digest. The observed per-source bound is reported beside it |
| search scope | catalogue size, 8 dihedral variants | **catalogue size N and the key(s) searched**; the per-query bound is N × N_K × 10⁻⁶ |
| key reference | none | **key id**, with a payload-derivation id (`e-img-m-1` HMAC rule). Never the key itself |
| abstention reason | `low-quality`, decode codes | `undecodable`, `unsupported-mode`, `too-small`. A mark adds "no mark decision possible" cases that a fingerprint lacks; none occur for the candidates on these inputs |
| embedding quality | none | **PSNR and the floor** of the marked artifact (an embedding-side field) |
| record resolution, signature, trust | outside the profile | outside the protocol; a decoded payload is never treated as authenticated |
| confidence | empty | empty: nothing is calibrated on natural images |

IMG.5 asks whether the IMGF and IMG results fit the lane 08 container fields, or whether a schema change must go through CONTRIBUTING. This protocol records the mapping above. **Its own reading: they fit the container's field list, but five fields have no home in any current file:** model digests, statistic name with n, threshold basis, key and payload-derivation id, and embedding quality. Any schema change is out of scope here and goes through CONTRIBUTING.

## 13. Statistical limits

All bounds are exact one-sided 95% Clopper–Pearson bounds and assume independent trials. The trials are not independent (§5): edits of one image are correlated, all images come from one generator, and every comparison under one key shares that key.

| Observation | What it bounds at most |
| --- | --- |
| 0 of 100 covers or 0 of 100 hosts accepted | per-source rate below 2.95% for that set, for this generator and these 100 payloads |
| 0 of 200 unmarked accepted (covers and hosts together) | below 1.49% per source |
| k of 100 for one condition | a 95% interval about ±4 to ±10 percentage points wide, depending on k |
| 0 accepts in C comparisons | consistent with the analytic ≤ C × 10⁻⁶. It does not measure it: that would need on the order of 10⁶ independent sources |

## 15. Changes that need a new protocol version

Any change to:
- the seed plan, image counts or generator;
- a scheme, a model file, the upstream sources, or scheme settings;
- the payload rule, keys, n or k;
- a condition's definition or encoder settings;
- W1–W6, P1–P4 or the PSNR floor;
- the record field sets;
- package versions or hashes, Python minor version, platform, or torch thread count;
- the abort rule.

These do **not** need a new version: notes outside the frozen paths; re-running after an abort; added analyses that are labelled post hoc and leave the verdicts unchanged (for example, re-reading `rawBits` at another k).

## 16. Needs the owner, and what the reviewer must check

**Needs the owner:**
- **UC-7 wording** (IMG.0), in [image-mark-use-case.md](image-mark-use-case.md).
- **A real-image set.** Synthetic images cannot show natural-image behaviour (§1). A small, licensed, non-private photo set (for example a few dozen CC0 or CC BY images whose licence permits modification) would be a second protocol version. Choosing and downloading it needs the owner (as IMGF.6 does for fingerprints).
- **SA-1B terms** for PixelSeal, if results are used to pick a carrier for distribution (lane 12).
- **WmForger or VAE regeneration** as stronger attacks: each needs another weight download and licence row.
- **GPU:** not needed. The budget fits one CPU laptop.

**The non-author reviewer should check, before the measured run:**
1. The digest: recompute it as described above, and compare it with the README.
2. That §5's false-accept argument is right, in particular that the bound is over the key, not over images, and what that means for clustered decoder outputs.
3. That P1–P4 are justified without data, and that nothing else should carry a pass line.
4. That the adapters (`twmark/schemes.py`) call upstream code faithfully. In particular: TrustMark raw bits against upstream `decode` (unit test), the replaced `check_and_download`, PixelSeal's `setup_model` path and the output quantisation, and the RGB/BGR swap for `dwtDct`.
5. That the stubs for `av` and `pycocotools` cannot hide a used code path. A use raises `StubUsed`.
6. That the conditions match §7 byte for byte (`twmark/attacks.py`, `run_mark.py`), and that the 12 edits are imported, not copied.
7. That no committed file holds an absolute home path, and that `summary.json` redacts paths.
8. The §11 statement that no experiment-range seed was generated: search the dry-test logs and tests for seeds 2,000,000–2,000,201.
9. That the code-identity check covers the imported `e-img-f-2` files.
10. The §12 mapping and the IMG.5 reading.
