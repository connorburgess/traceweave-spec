**UC-7 (proposed) — Recognize a marked image.** A creator marks still images that they may modify, keeps the original, the marked artifact digest, the mark profile and a private catalogue, and publishes the marked image. Later, they check a candidate image they can read locally. The check never uploads the candidate.

- **What is embedded.** TrustMark-Q carries 61 payload bits, BCH-coded into 100 transmitted bits (35 error-correction bits and 4 fixed version bits). PixelSeal carries 256 payload bits. The payload is HMAC-SHA256(creator key, entry id), truncated. It is a keyed locator for a private catalogue entry. It is not an identity claim, a signature or a licence.
- **Key handling.** One secret random key per creator, stored outside any repository and never placed in a record. Records carry a key id and the payload-derivation rule id. Rotating keys multiplies the search scope (below).
- **Decision.** A decode by itself is not presence evidence ([lane 10](../lanes/10-evidence-review.md#after-the-report-audit): blind BCH success occurs on 5.9–15.3% of unmarked images in X1). The verifier counts agreement m between decoded raw bits and each expected payload, **only on bit positions that are direct keyed-hash output**, and accepts an entry iff m ≥ k. For TrustMark these are the 61 data positions: its error-correction bits depend on the data, and its version bits are the same in every mark, so neither can count as independent evidence. For PixelSeal they are all 256 payload bits. k is the smallest value with P(Bin(n, ½) ≥ k) ≤ 10⁻⁶: **49 of 61** (TrustMark, 9.85 × 10⁻⁷) or **167 of 256** (PixelSeal, 6.22 × 10⁻⁷). Over the key's randomness, that is the per-comparison bound for an image nobody marked with this payload, whatever its content. Searching N entries under N_K keys gives at most N × N_K × 10⁻⁶ per query. Outcomes: `match`, `ambiguous` (two or more entries accepted), `no-match`, or an abstention with a reason (`undecodable`, `unsupported-mode`, `too-small`).
- **What a detection can and cannot evidence.** The decoders and encoders are public and use no key. Anyone can decode a marked image's bits and embed them into any other image, with no key and no access to the catalogue. The copy then passes the same test as the original. So an accepted detection shows only that **bits matching this entry's payload were embedded in the candidate**. It does not show who embedded them. It does not show that the candidate is the original, or derives from it. It does not show that the creator marked this particular image. The false-accept bound above covers chance agreement, never deliberate copying. Combining a mark with other evidence (exact digests, a fingerprint match to the original, a signed record of what was published) is a separate step, and even that does not prove ancestry.
- **Quality.** A marked output below the profile's PSNR floor (38 dB in the first protocol) is refused, not published.
- **Separate results.** Detection, record resolution, signature validity and trust stay separate. Mark evidence and fingerprint evidence are never multiplied.

Expected misses: deep crop, rotation, flip for some schemes, heavy recompression, model-based or author-supplied removal, and re-marking by another party. Expected false acceptances (accepted, but not evidence of origin): a mark re-embedded by anyone without the key, and a residual transplanted from a marked image to an unrelated one. Every removal and forgery count in the study is a lower bound on what an attacker can do. Synthetic-image results say nothing about photographs.

## UC-7 verification plan (proposed)

- **Checked:** marks embed and are recovered offline on CPU from pinned code and weights; the implementation of the expected-payload test rejects unmarked images, wrong keys and wrong entries; survival under 12 edits and 5 removal attempts is counted; transplant, keyless re-embedding and overwrite outcomes are counted; quality of the marked output is measured; records carry the fields the shared record needs.
- **By:** IMG.1 (pins and licences: [lane 12](../lanes/12-image-mark-licences.md), `weights.json`, `sources.json`, `requirements.txt`), IMG.2 and IMG.4 (the measured run of [protocol `e-img-m-2`](image-mark-protocol.md)), IMG.3 (that protocol frozen with its digest before running), IMG.5 (its §12 record mapping).
- **Pass:** each item is decided by an exit code or a count.
  - `fetch_weights.py --verify` and `fetch_sources.py --verify` exit 0 (6/6 and 84/84 files).
  - The unit tests exit 0.
  - `run_mark.py full` exits 0 twice under the network-deny sandbox, with the probe refused (W2).
  - `run_mark.py compare` exits 0 (W3).
  - `recordValidation.errors` has 0 entries (W4).
  - For TrustMark-Q and PixelSeal separately: clean marked images correctly matched ≥ 95/100 (P1). Implementation checks: unmarked accepted 0 in 20,000 comparisons (P2), wrong key 0 in 10,000 (P3), wrong entry 0 in 9,900 (P4). These are close to guaranteed by construction; they do not measure a false-accept rate or forgery resistance.
  - `compare` exits 0. Exit 5 (an invalid pair with equal inputs) means a rerun under the protocol's rule, not a fail.
  - Every report-only count (edits, removal, transplant, re-embedding, overwrite, quality) appears with its denominator of 100.
- **Fail:** any of those commands exits non-zero, except `compare` exit 5, which is handled by the rerun rule; any record error; P1 below 95; any accept in P2–P4 (that scheme is recorded as negative-result, most likely an implementation fault); any count without a denominator. Transplant, re-embedding and removal successes are **not** failures of the plan. They are recorded limits of the use case.
- **Today:** pins and licences are recorded. The unit tests pass (22/22). Smoke dry runs on 3 smoke images per scheme run offline and are byte-identical across runs. They are not results. **Needs:** a non-author review of the frozen protocol, then the measured run; the owner's acceptance of this wording.
- **Unverifiable:** who embedded a detected mark, or whether the candidate is the original; behaviour on natural photographs (synthetic images only; a real-image set needs the owner); a false-accept rate near 10⁻⁶ (analytic only; 0 of 200 unmarked bounds the per-source rate below about 1.5%); resistance to adaptive, learned or regeneration attacks not in the protocol; ownership, authorship or permission; any result across hosts.
